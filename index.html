I spend a lot of time thinking about brains, learning, and the implications of technology. Sometimes those thoughts become research, projects, and writing.

About me

I’m a PhD student in NeuroAI at Yale, working at the intersection of causal inference, manifold learning, and theoretical neuroscience. My research asks: how can we use generative models of neural data to uncover the mechanisms that guide the brain?

I studied computer science and pure mathematics at Columbia University, where I did research on manifold inference.

Research

I use mathematics and computational tools to study brains and artificial systems. I’m interested in how they learn and change over time, and what their structure can tell us about how they work.

Decoding Alignment without Encoding Alignment: A critique of similarity analysis in neuroscience

J. Bertram, L. Dyballa, T. A. Keller, S. Kinger, S. W. Zucker

2026 · arXiv preprint

Two neural systems can look alike on standard similarity measures while organizing information very differently. We show why matching their outputs is only part of the story, and how studying the organization of neurons reveals what those measures miss.

Inferring Active Neural Circuits Using Diffusion Scores

S. Kinger, J. Bertram, L. Dyballa, E. Yemini, S. W. Zucker

2026 · arXiv preprint

Can recordings of brain activity tell us which neurons influence one another? We use diffusion models to infer candidate connections and their timing, then compare them with known wiring in the nervous system of C. elegans.

How ‘Neural’ is a Neural Foundation Model?

J. Bertram, L. Dyballa, T. A. Keller, S. Kinger, S. W. Zucker

2026 · arXiv preprint

A model can predict brain activity without working like a brain. We look inside a neural foundation model to see how it processes visual information, finding both parallels with biology and differences hidden by prediction accuracy alone.
